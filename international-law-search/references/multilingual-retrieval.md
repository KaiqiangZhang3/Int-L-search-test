# Multilingual Retrieval

## Core Contract

Treat every substantive language as an independent retrieval branch. A branch exists to find materials, concepts, institutions, or perspectives that another language branch may not expose. Design it from the relevant legal and scholarly vocabulary; do not merely translate another branch's query list.

There is **no fixed English baseline**. Select languages from the approved research purpose, relevant legal systems and institutions, available access, and the user's choices. English may be one branch, several branches may run without English, and no language is inherently the primary branch.

## Required Branch Record

Before retrieval, record these fields for each substantive language:

| Field | Required content |
| --- | --- |
| Research purpose | The distinct retrieval contribution expected from this language |
| Local vocabulary | Local legal terms, concepts, names, spelling variants, and synonyms—not translated keywords alone |
| Platforms | Language-appropriate databases, repositories, catalogs, journals, and official sources |
| Access assumptions | Institutional, subscription, open-access, local-file, and likely full-text conditions |
| Budget | Query, candidate, and full-text-review limits for this branch; add time or platform limits when material |
| Authority relationship | The official-translation status: whether texts are authentic, institution-issued official translations, unofficial translations, scholarship, or commentary, and how the versions relate |
| Actual contribution | Candidates and reviewed texts added, distinct positions or source types added, and duplicates encountered |
| Remaining gap | Unsearched platforms, inaccessible works, weak vocabulary coverage, missing perspectives, or other limits |

Confirm substantive language branches and their budgets in the scope card. Report actual contribution and remaining gap at the checkpoint. A branch that produces no useful material is still reportable evidence about the search, not a reason to conceal the attempt.

## Auxiliary Verification

An auxiliary language may be used without a separate retrieval budget only for isolated verification, such as confirming a party name, official title, citation, or translation against a known source. If work in that language expands to discovery, candidate screening, or repeated queries, pause and ask the user to approve it as a substantive branch with its own purpose and budget.

## Text and Translation Authority

Record the status of every relied-on text: authentic language version, institution-issued official translation, publisher translation, or unofficial translation. Do not infer authority from the interface language or host domain alone. When versions diverge, preserve the relevant versions, identify the discrepancy, and avoid resolving it beyond the reviewed evidence.

## Reporting Rule

Compare branches by their stated purpose and actual contribution, not by raw source count alone. One non-English source does not establish representative multilingual, regional, Global South, or perspective coverage.
