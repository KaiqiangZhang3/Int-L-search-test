# International-Law Source Records

## Record the Source Before Ranking It

Normalize each item according to its source type. Keep the formatted citation separate from structured metadata. The generic `publication` field names the publication or forum; it must not contain an entire citation.

## Type-Specific Details

- Cases and decisions: court or tribunal, case number, decision date, procedural stage, parties, jurisdictional context, and relevant paragraphs.
- Treaties and instruments: adoption, signature, entry into force, depositary, status date, and the parties or applicability relevant to the question.
- International-organization documents: organization, organ, document number, session, version, addressees, and status date.
- Journal articles: journal, volume, issue, pages, and DOI in the identifier field.
- Books and chapters: publisher, edition, place, editors, book title, ISBN in the identifier field, and chapter pages.
- Working papers: series or institution, number, and version date.
- Institutional reports: issuing institution, report number, and status date.
- Commentary: outlet, publication date, and its editorial or practitioner context.

## Legal Status Is Contextual

Do not use a global `binding` boolean. Record the source category, issuing authority, addressees or parties, procedural posture, jurisdictional context, and status date needed to understand legal significance. Distinguish binding law, interpretive material, soft law, policy, scholarship, and commentary in the reader-facing explanation without assuming that one label answers every jurisdictional question.

For corporate-role research, keep international legal personality, subject status, procedural standing, direct rights, and direct obligations separate. Do not collapse corporate rights under investment law into corporate obligations in business and human rights. Include domestic corporate-governance literature only when the connection to an approved international norm is explicit.

## Versions and Links

Record official, publisher, open-access, database, and other stable links separately. Use version relationships for original, revised, consolidated, corrected, translated, superseded, implemented, protocol, annex, and later interpretation-stage materials. A working paper and a final article may be linked without silently deleting either record. Likewise, distinguish a draft instrument from a final instrument and a judgment from later correction, interpretation, or implementation stages.

Normalize title capitalization before conflict detection. Capitalization alone is not an identity conflict. Preserve materially different titles, identifiers, dates, or issuers as attributed conflict candidates until resolved.

## Commentary

High-quality international-law blogs and practitioner analysis may appear as a separate commentary class when useful for current developments, debate mapping, or procedural explanation. Label them as commentary and record their review extent. Do not present them as binding authority or peer-reviewed scholarship. Ordinary web pages remain discovery leads unless they are themselves the research object.
